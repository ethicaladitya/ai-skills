# Comparison and alternatives pages

Readers on these pages are close to a decision, and they will check your claims. Honesty is the strategy.

## Formats
| Format | Query | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| `<Competitor> alternative` | switching from one tool | why people leave it · you as the option · detailed comparison · who should switch and who shouldn't · how to switch · CTA |
| `<Competitor> alternatives` / `best <category> tools` | early research | selection criteria · 4–7 real options (you included, not always first) · summary table · a short honest take on each · pick-by-use-case · CTA |
| `<You> vs <Competitor>` | head-to-head | 2–3 line verdict · at-a-glance table · section per dimension · who each is best for · CTA |
| `<A> vs <B>` (neither is you) | comparing two others | fair comparison of both · where a third option fits · table with all three |

## Every page
- **Verdict first:** a 2–3 sentence TL;DR for scanners and AI answers.
- **Paragraphs, not just tables:** for each dimension, explain the difference and when it matters.
- **"Who it's for":** say plainly when the competitor is the better choice.
- **Disclose** that you make one of the products.
- **Date the facts:** "Prices and features checked on <date>". Link to competitors' own pages for their claims.

## Data discipline
- Keep one data file per competitor (positioning, pricing, key features, strengths, weaknesses, best
  for, common complaints, source URL and check date). Every page reads from it, so an update fixes every page.
- Verify on the competitor's own site (or by using the product) on the day you write. Mine public reviews
  for real strengths and complaints, and quote them fairly.
- Re-verify pricing and major features every quarter, and fully refresh once a year.
- Never invent prices, features, limits or customer quotes. Never disparage, and don't use their logos.

## SEO
- One page per comparison intent; titles like "<A> vs <B>: <honest one-line verdict> (<Year>)".
- A hub page linking every comparison; comparisons linked from the relevant feature pages.
- Visible FAQ ("Is <X> free?", "What is the best alternative to <X>?"). Google no longer shows FAQ rich
  results, so FAQPage schema is optional.
- These pages get cited in AI answers; being *recommended* also depends on what others say about you (see authority-building).
