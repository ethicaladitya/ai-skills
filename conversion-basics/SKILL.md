# Conversion basics

Work top-down; earlier items usually matter more.

1. **Value proposition in 5 seconds.** Can a cold visitor say what this is, who it's for and why it's
   better? Use the customer's words, benefits over features, one main message.
2. **Headline.** Specific and outcome-led ("Fast, secure WordPress sites for Indian businesses, built in
   4–6 weeks"), matching the query or ad that brought people here.
3. **One primary CTA,** visible without scrolling and repeated at decision points. The label says what
   happens ("Get a free site audit", "Book a 20-minute call"), not "Submit".
4. **Trust near the CTA:** real client logos, attributed testimonials, case studies with verifiable
   outcomes, review scores, credentials. Only real ones. Never invent clients or numbers.
5. **Objections answered on the page:** price ("what does it cost?", given as ranges or cost factors),
   fit ("will this work for my business?"), effort, risk (process, timeline, support, guarantees you
   actually offer). An FAQ and a "how we work" section do this well.
6. **Friction:** as few form fields as possible (name, email, message; everything else optional), clear
   next steps after submitting, a working mobile layout, fast loading, `tel:` and WhatsApp links where relevant.
7. **Scannability:** clear visual hierarchy, short sections, whitespace, images that support the message.

## By page type
- **Home:** clear positioning plus paths for both ready-to-buy and still-researching visitors.
- **Service page:** feature to benefit, use cases, process, pricing guidance, proof, CTA.
- **Blog post:** a contextual CTA matched to the topic, inline at a natural stopping point, not a generic banner.
- **Landing page:** the message matches its source, there's one CTA, and optional navigation is removed.

## Measure before and after
A lead is a GA4 key event (e.g. form submit or `generate_lead`), so check it fires before changing
anything. Track the conversion rate per page. Change one thing at a time on low-traffic sites, and give
each change enough time; with little traffic, treat results as directional, not proof.
