# Local and regional SEO

## Decide the business type first
- **Service-area business** (serves clients remotely or on site, no walk-in office): no public address
  needed; use `areaServed` and service pages. Most agencies are this.
- **Physical location:** full address, map and opening hours.
- **Both:** a real office page plus service pages for the wider area.

## Listings (the owner creates the accounts; the agent prepares the content)
- **Google Business Profile:** the right primary category matters most, plus a few accurate secondary
  categories, real photos, services and a description. Link it to a relevant page, not always the homepage.
- **Bing Places** (feeds Bing, Copilot and assistants that use Bing) and **Apple Business Connect.**
- **NAP consistency:** name, address and phone identical on the site footer and contact page, in the schema
  and on every listing. Check all three and fix any mismatch.

## Pages
- One strong page per core service. City or region pages only where there is something genuinely
  local to say (clients, projects, local specifics).
- **Swap test:** if you can swap the city name and the page still reads fine, it's a doorway page.
  Rewrite it with real local substance or don't publish it.
- A national page and a city page can coexist: the national page targets "<service> company india",
  the city page "<service> in bhopal". Link them both ways and make their titles clearly different so they
  don't compete.
- City and service in the title and H1 of local pages; a click-to-call `tel:` link; the contact form near the top.

## Reviews
- Ask real clients for reviews with a direct link, soon after a good outcome. Reply to every review.
- **Never gate reviews** (pre-screening happy customers before sending them to Google) and never buy or
  fake them. Both break Google's policies.

## Schema
`ProfessionalService` or a `LocalBusiness` subtype with name, url, telephone, `areaServed` (named cities,
regions or the country), `address` only if it's public, `geo` with 5+ decimals if it's a physical
location, and `sameAs` to the listings.

## Authority
Local chambers of commerce and startup ecosystems, local press, event sponsorships, and inclusion in
genuine "best <service> in <city>" roundups (earned, not bought).
